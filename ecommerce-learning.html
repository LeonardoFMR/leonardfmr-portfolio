E-commerce ecommerce Notes

Concepts and practical notes from my e-commerce ecommerce journey.

1. Inbound E-commerce Strategy

Inbound marketing is a methodology focused on attracting customers through valuable content and personalized experiences that respond to their needs and behavior.

The Inbound Methodology

  • Attract: Bring potential customers to your online store.
  • Engage: Help visitors make purchasing decisions.
  • Delight: Create positive experiences that encourage customer loyalty.

Key Performance Indicators (KPIs)

  • Website traffic
  • E-commerce conversion rate
  • Average order value (AOV)
  • Customer lifetime value (CLV)
  • Online revenue

2. Understanding Your Buyers

Buyer Persona

A buyer persona represents an ideal customer based on real data and informed assumptions, including age, income, education, nationality and technical ability.

RFM Customer Segmentation

RFM analyzes purchasing behavior using three factors:

  • Recency: How recently did a customer purchase?
  • Frequency: How often do they purchase?
  • Monetary: How much do they spend?

Customer Buying Types

  • Competitive: Logic-driven customers looking for the best available option.
  • Spontaneous: Customers influenced by convenience and emotions.
  • Methodical: Customers who carefully research before purchasing.
  • Humanistic: Customers who value personal relationships and emotional connections.

Competitive and spontaneous buyers tend to have shorter purchasing cycles, while methodical and humanistic buyers generally take longer to decide.

3. Planning and Marketing Automation

An e-commerce marketing plan should define important milestones, schedule promotional offers and identify opportunities for automated customer communication.

Marketing Automation Workflows

  • Abandoned carts: Send reminder emails to customers who leave without purchasing.
  • Post-purchase follow-ups: Collect feedback and improve customer experience.
  • Birthday campaigns: Reconnect with customers through personalized messages.
  • Win-back campaigns: Re-engage customers who have stopped purchasing.

4. Attracting Visitors to Your Store

Search Engine Optimization (SEO)

Product pages should be optimized to help search engines understand their content and make products easier to find.

  • Create individual pages for each product.
  • Include relevant keywords in URLs, titles and descriptions.
  • Use H1 and H2 headings to organize product information.
  • Include descriptive alternative text for product images.
  • Use structured product information such as prices and reviews.

Content Marketing

  • Blogging: Share useful information and guide visitors toward relevant products.
  • Seasonal content: Create shopping guides around holidays and special occasions.
  • User-generated content: Encourage reviews and ratings to build customer confidence.
  • Social media: Create consistent content that represents the brand.
  • Paid media: Use search advertising, display advertising and retargeting campaigns.

5. Improving Engagement and Conversions

Catalog Optimization

Catalog optimization involves analyzing product performance to identify opportunities to improve sales and reduce friction in the purchasing process.

  • Low traffic, high conversion: Products to promote.
  • High traffic, high conversion: Top-selling products.
  • Low traffic, low conversion: Products to consider removing.
  • High traffic, low conversion: Products to optimize.

Conversion Optimization Techniques

  • A/B testing: Compare different versions of a webpage or email to identify which performs better.
  • Site search: Analyze customer searches, product clicks and purchases.
  • Email marketing: Capture email addresses to build personalized communication.
  • Customer support: Offer accessible contact methods to answer questions.
  • Merchandising: Use strategies such as free-shipping thresholds to increase order value.
  • Remarketing: Reconnect with interested visitors through targeted advertising.

6. Customer Satisfaction and Retention

Customer satisfaction depends on the entire shopping experience, including fulfillment, delivery, communication and post-purchase support.

Post-Purchase Experience

  • Provide accessible order tracking information.
  • Create personalized delivery experiences.
  • Request product reviews and customer feedback.
  • Recommend complementary products through upselling.
  • Offer relevant discounts and loyalty programs.
  • Review email lists and identify inactive customers.

7. HubSpot and Shopify Integration

The course explains how integrating Shopify with HubSpot can connect e-commerce information with customer relationship management and marketing workflows.

Data Synchronization

  • Sync product information, including prices, categories and vendors.
  • Sync customer records and identify existing contacts using email addresses.
  • Associate orders with customers and products when contact information is available.
  • Orders without email addresses may remain unassociated with a contact.

Customer Segmentation

Customer lists can be segmented using purchase amounts, purchased products, quantities and customer tags.

E-commerce Workflows

  • Welcome new customers.
  • Recover abandoned carts.
  • Re-engage inactive customers.

Reporting and Dashboards
